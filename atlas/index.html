01 · The gist

The proof in three sentences

The argument has one hard estimate and two well-understood transformations of it. Read the three cards from the arithmetic to the destination; every term in them is a component of the map below.

Arithmetic supplies a one-sided bound

For every primitive character $\psi$ and every small $\varepsilon$, build families on which a positive combination of centered prime sums satisfies $G_P(x)\le x^{1/2+9\varepsilon}$ at every point of arbitrarily late windows $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$. The tools: chains of about $\sqrt x$ theta lifts, Galois representations attached to automorphic forms, Iwasawa-type bounds for Galois cohomology.

Analysis turns the bound into a zero-free half-plane

The Mellin transform turns a zero $\rho$ into a pole with residue $-S(\rho)/(\rho(\rho-1))$, $S(\rho)>0$. High derivatives aimed at the windows would see that pole. Choosing $\theta$ after the zero and $\varepsilon$ after $\theta$, no zero survives to the right of $\tfrac12$.

Reflection delivers the critical line

Euler factors pass from primitive to all characters, and the functional equations reflect the half-plane onto the line: Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis and Dirichlet GRH for every positive modulus.

Where the difficulty lives. The analytic engine and the closing deductions are machine-checked with the window estimate as an explicit hypothesis (two verified cuts). The estimate itself comes from the arithmetic supply. Its terms belong to the complete proof, which two independent kernels accepted and several independent builders replayed (see Verification); in this atlas they are read at their pinned source lines, and only some of them were also checked one by one.

04 · Illustrated ideas

Eleven pictures of the argument

Amber tags mark figures computed from definitions (every number asserted by the build); grey tags mark schematic diagrams; green marks facts also proved in this repository's Lean library.

computed

The quantity the proof must control

The archive's statistic is $G(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)(y/n-1)-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y$. By the explicit formula, $G(y)-\log 2\pi=-\sum_\rho y^\rho/(\rho(\rho-1))$ up to $O(y^{-2})$. The amber curve is computed from the primes, the indigo one from the first 100 zeros (computed here by Euler–Maclaurin summation); on $[10^2, 2\cdot10^6]$ they differ by at most $0.00064\sqrt y$. A zero at $\beta+i\gamma$ off the line would add oscillations of size $y^{\beta-1/2}$ (dashed: a hypothetical zero at $0.62+40i$). The proof needs only an upper bound for $G$, and only on windows $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$.

schematiccomputed

Only the right half-plane matters

If $L(\rho,\chi)=0$ in the strip, the functional equation gives $L(1-\rho,\chi^{-1})=0$; with complex conjugation this is the mirror image of $\rho$ in the critical line. So it suffices to clear $\operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12$ for every character. The dots are the first 10 zeta zeros, computed.

computed

Ramps make the transform exact

Each prime power switches on a ramp $\Lambda(n)(y/n-1)_+$ at $y=n$. The Mellin transform of one ramp is $n^{-s}/(s(s-1))$ (checked numerically at 9 points, error at most 3.0e-10). Summed, the ramps produce $-L'/L$; the centring terms remove the pole at $s=1$, and what remains hovers around $\log 2\pi$, the residue at $s=0$.

schematic

Zeros cannot hide in a positive family

At a common zero $\rho$ each member of a positive family contributes a positive multiple of $-1/(\rho(\rho-1))$ to the residue, so the contributions point the same way and add up to $S(\rho)>0$ times that number. With signed weights two zeros could cancel; the archive requires every weight $a_i>0$.

computed

High derivatives are a spotlight

The $k$-th derivative of the Laplace transform weights $g(u)=G_F(e^u)$ by $u^k e^{-\sigma u}/k!$, a bump at $u=k/\sigma$ whose relative width shrinks like $k^{-1/2}$. Aimed at the window $[U,1.5U]$ with $k=\lceil 1.25\,\sigma U\rceil$ it puts 68.0%, 84.0%, 95.2%, 99.5% of its mass inside for $U=20,40,80,160$. A pole at distance $d$ contributes about $d^{-k}$; the window bound allows only $e^{\eta U}r^{-k}$ with $r>d$, which is impossible once $k\ge U$ and $\eta$ is small.

schematic

The order of choices

The losses $e_D=2$, $e_L=0$ are fixed first. Given a zero with $\operatorname{Re}\rho=\beta>\tfrac12$, take $\theta=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\min(\beta-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ and then $\varepsilon$ with $\tfrac12+9\varepsilon\le\theta$; the arithmetic supplies windows for that $\varepsilon$, and the criterion excludes the zero. A bound at $\varepsilon=0$ is never needed.

exact

Where the square root, and every ε, is paid

The geometric discrepancy has a cost $x/m$ and a cost $m$; with $m=x^a$ their larger exponent $\max(1-a,a)$ is smallest at $a=\tfrac12$ — the announcement's "chain of length $\sqrt X$". The staircase adds the exact losses of the finite comparison: $\tfrac{13}{8}+(e_D+e_L+3)+1+\tfrac38+1=e_D+e_L+7$, which is $9$ for the archive's $e_D=2$, $e_L=0$.

computedformal

One interval survives every exceptional twist

An $\ell$-adic determinant estimate leaves at most $R$ centres, and each centre accounts for at most one exceptional twist (ultrametric separation, proved in this repository's Lean library). Each exceptional twist forbids a logarithmic interval $[a,Ba)$, and the induction of window1 finds $[y,2y]$ avoiding all of them. The reading project's example — twists $2^{16}$ and $2^{180}$, $C_t=2$, $\eta=1$, $X=2^{128}$ — gives $[Y,Y^2]=[2^{16},2^{32}]$.

computedformal

No three in a line: Behrend spheres

Integer vectors of fixed norm, written in a base large enough to avoid carries, satisfy no short affine relation, because a line meets a sphere at most twice. The six permutations of (0,1,2) in base 24 pass all 1,440 relation checks; the general statement is BehrendReading.sphere_rel, compiled in this repository.

schematic

A chain of about √x theta lifts

Each theta lift $U(X_j)\to U(X_{j+1})$ is meant to mimic one order of multiplicity in Stepanov's method. The archive's RankVal fixes the length between $\sqrt{x\delta_1}$ and $2\sqrt{x\delta_1}$, $\delta_1=x^{-\varepsilon/8}$; denominators clear by $\prod_j D_j$ (ChainInt).

schematic

A Galois representation by quadratic descent

For a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal $\pi$ on $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over a CM field $E$, representations over infinitely many $K=E(\sqrt{-p})$ are patched, through index-two trace identities, into one $\rho$ over $E$ whose Frobenius polynomials match the Satake parameters of $\pi$ at all but finitely many places.

06 · The architecture, compiled

A Lean skeleton of the whole argument

This repository's Lean library (Lean 4.35.0, no imports) states every component as an explicit hypothesis over abstract types and proves only how they compose — the shape of the proof, checked. A toy model with zeros on the line shows the hypotheses are jointly satisfiable. It is a map, not a proof of RH.

The arithmetic supply, as the single hypothesis

/-- The arithmetic supply, in the form consumed by `zeroFreeAll_of_topG`. For each primitive `ψ`
and each admissible small `ε` there is a family containing `ψ` whose prime sum satisfies the
window bound with exponent `loss ε = 1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 7)·ε`, where `e_D = 2`, `e_L = 0` were
fixed **before** `ε`. In the archive this is produced from `TopG` and `VWinAt ψ 401` (through
`supplyChi`, `core_gm` and `topDesWin`). It is the one non-classical input. -/
structure Supply (P : Plane) (E : Engine P) where
  Eps : Type
  Small : Eps → Prop
  loss : Eps → E.Exp
  family : P.Char → Eps → E.Family
  mem_family : ∀ ψ ε, E.Mem ψ (family ψ ε)
  windows : ∀ ψ ε, P.Primitive ψ → Small ε → E.Windows (family ψ ε) (loss ε)
  /-- θ after the zero, ε after θ (`level_of_tiedWin`): for `Re s > 1/2` take
  `θ = 1/2 + min(Re s - 1/2, 1/2)/2`, so that `Re s > θ`, then a small `ε` with `loss ε ≤ θ`.
  This is possible because `loss ε → 1/2` as `ε → 0` with the losses fixed. -/
  choose : ∀ s, P.Right s → ∃ θ, E.RightOf s θ ∧ ∃ ε, Small ε ∧ E.Le (loss ε) θ

08 · Glossary

Words that carry weight here

Centered prime sum
$G(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)(y/n-1)-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y$; its twisted versions $g_\chi$ add $y\,(L'/L)(1,\chi)$ instead of the main terms.
Power window
All $y$ with $Y\le y\le Y^{\lambda}$; the archive uses $\lambda=3/2$ for the prime sums and $[Y,Y^2]$ for the cohomology.
One-sided bound
An upper bound $G_F(y)\le Cy^\theta$ at every point of a window; no lower bound is required.
Positive family
A finite family of characters with weights $a_i>0$ (principal member only of modulus one); residues at common zeros add up.
Primitive character
A Dirichlet character at its conductor; every character is induced by a unique primitive one.
Theta lift
Transfer of automorphic forms between groups by a theta kernel; the archive chains about $\sqrt x$ of them.
Galois row
Row0b: the local–global compatibility package attaching $\ell$-adic Galois representations to regular algebraic polarized cuspidal automorphic representations over CM fields.
Cohomological length
The $\mathbb Z_\ell$-length of the locally trivial part of $H^1$ of a twisted module; VWinAt bounds weighted sums of these lengths.
Verified cut
An implication machine-checked against the archive with the remaining inputs as explicit hypotheses.
Axiom audit
Lean's list of axioms a declaration depends on; the audits permit only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
Source-read
A declaration located at pinned source lines and read, but not separately replayed by this audit.
Pinned link
A URL into the public mirror at commit 40e56bab, whose tree is byte-identical to the archive.

  • ramp. Computed. Left: the weighted ramps Lambda(n)(y/n-1)_+ for prime powers n <= 13, each switching on at y = n with slope Lambda(n)/n. Right: their full sum minus y log y - (1+gamma) y, i.e. G(y), for 1 <= y <= 120; it hovers around log 2pi (dashed), the residue at s = 0.

  • balance. Left: exponents 1-a of x/m and a of m when m = x^a; the larger is smallest at a = 1/2. Right: exact staircase of the losses in units of epsilon, from the finite comparison to 1/2 + (e_D+e_L+7) eps = 1/2 + 9 eps for the source's choice e_D = 2, e_L = 0.
